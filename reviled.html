Reviled

“Blessed are those who are persecuted because of righteousness,
for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. Blessed are you when people insult you,
persecute you and falsely say allkinds of evil against you because of me.”
—Matthew 5:11

What the blessing was,
she didn’t know anymore,
sitting at her desk reviewing the minutes from the meeting.
Five members at the church had accused her
of financial mismanagement, poor makeup, friendliness toward homosexuals,
and not writing enough thank you notes.
She didn’t recognize the person they described.
She saw in her mind the stony faces reading prepared statements,
the church elder looking down at the table,
the association president tapping a pencil on a blank piece of paper,
the committee staring silently as it listened to the testimony against her.
What had she done to deserve this?
All she wanted to do was help people follow Jesus.
All the years spent preaching the scriptures
the weddings, baptisms and funerals
to mark seasons, years and passages—
they all evaporated like rain falling on burning desert sands.

It was the end of her pastorate.
She knew by the molten mass of depression
that was forming, even now, between her eyes.
She looked at the cross hanging on her office wall,
a crucifix, actually— some thing she picked up in Guatemala years ago.
When she began her ministry she remembered saying to God, “Use me.”
“But not like this,” she whispered now. “I didn’t mean anything like this.”
Jesus looked down on her,
his face mangled by betrayals&mdash
Judas who turned him over to the temple officials,
Peter and the other disciples fleeing at the first sign of trouble,
the taunts and blows of the Roman guards.
And then the final betrayal—
God abandoning him to death after all.
Behold the man who blessed persecution,
hanging from a cross.
Did he know what he was saying then,
on that happy mountain looking down at the adoring crowds?
Did she?

There was more to the story, of course.
She believed in Christ’s resurrection from the dead.
The end of Jesus’ ministry on
earth would not be the last word,
nor would it be hers.
But she knew now what she didn’t know then:
A cross lay along every journey to God.
And she had found hers,
erected by the very people she had served for 12 years.
She stared at her Jesus,
blurry now through the distorted lens of years,
and whispered, “Jesus, forgive them. They know not what they do.”
And then she broke down sobbing,
